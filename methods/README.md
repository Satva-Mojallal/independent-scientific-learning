Experimental Methods

This folder contains notes about experimental and analytical methods discussed during the learning project.

The aim is to understand not only how a method works, but also why a researcher would choose it, what it measures, and what conclusions its results can support.

Structure of a method note

For each method, aim to document:

1. Purpose: What biological or analytical question can the method address?
2. Principle: What is the underlying scientific principle?
3. Procedure overview: What are the main stages of the method?
4. Measurement: What is directly detected or measured?
5. Interpretation: What conclusions can the results support?
6. Limitations: What can the method not establish on its own?
7. Controls: Which controls are necessary, and why?
8. Sources of error: What technical or biological factors could affect the result?
9. Complementary methods: What other method could strengthen or validate the conclusion?
