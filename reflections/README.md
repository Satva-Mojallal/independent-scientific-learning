Learning and Researcher Development Reflections

This folder contains occasional reflections on my development as an independent learner and scientific thinker.

The purpose is to evaluate whether the learning process is improving my ability to understand biology, apply scientific knowledge, and reason through experimental problems.

Possible reflection topics

- What scientific concepts have become easier to explain?
- Which methods can I now select more confidently, and why?
- Can I identify appropriate controls for an unfamiliar experiment?
- Can I distinguish association from direct binding?
- Can I distinguish protein abundance from phosphorylation state?
- Can I recognize when a conclusion is stronger than the available evidence?
- Can I propose useful follow-up experiments?
- Can I retrieve and apply knowledge after a period without studying it?
- Can I transfer what I have learned to a new biological question?
- Which areas still require deeper study?

Periodic evaluation

At appropriate intervals, I will revisit earlier questions and experimental scenarios.

The purpose is to determine whether I can still explain the underlying principles and apply them without relying entirely on previous explanations.

Important principle

Progress should be evaluated by the quality of scientific explanations, experimental decisions, and interpretation—not by the number of documents, papers, or notes accumulated.

The aim is to become a more capable scientific thinker through consistent learning, critical reasoning, and honest reflection.
