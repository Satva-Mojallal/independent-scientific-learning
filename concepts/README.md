Biological Concepts

This folder contains explanations of biological concepts that connect different experiments, methods, and research questions.

The aim is to develop an understanding of the underlying biology rather than memorizing isolated definitions.

Potential topics from earlier learning

Protein interactions

- Protein-protein association versus direct physical binding.
- Protein complexes and how interactions can be investigated experimentally.
- What co-immunoprecipitation and pull-down assays can and cannot demonstrate.

Protein expression and signalling

- Total protein abundance versus phosphorylation state.
- Receptor signalling.
- EGFR and phosphorylated EGFR (pEGFR).
- Interpreting changes in protein expression and activation.

Cell behaviour

- Vimentin and its relationship to cellular behaviour.
- Cell migration.
- The relationship between molecular changes and cellular phenotypes.

Cell death and measurement

- Cell-death-associated measurements.
- Annexin V and propidium iodide staining.
- Interpretation of flow-cytometry populations.

Protein analysis

- Protein solubility.
- Protein extraction and sample preparation.
- The relationship between sample preparation and analytical results.

Molecular biology

- DNA strand orientation.
- PCR amplification.
- Forward and reverse primers.
- Primer-defined amplicon boundaries.

How to develop each concept

A useful concept note should answer:

1. What is the concept?
2. What is its biological significance?
3. What mechanisms are involved?
4. How can it be investigated experimentally?
5. What methods can measure it?
6. What evidence would support a proposed mechanism?
7. What alternative explanations should be considered?

The goal is to connect biological understanding with experimental reasoning.
