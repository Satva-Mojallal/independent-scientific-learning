1. Purpose

Before this repository was created, I had already worked through a range of molecular and cellular biology concepts, laboratory methods, and experimental-reasoning questions in guided learning sessions.

I did not consistently document those sessions at the time.

This file preserves the main areas that can be identified from the available learning history. It is deliberately retrospective. It does not assign invented dates, claim that every topic was mastered, or suggest that the experiments were personally performed.

2. Molecular and cellular biology themes

Topics discussed included:

- Protein interactions and the difference between association and direct physical binding.
- Protein expression and the distinction between total protein abundance and phosphorylation state.
- Signalling-related measurements, including EGFR and phosphorylated EGFR (pEGFR).
- Vimentin and its discussion in the context of cell behaviour and migration.
- The relationship between molecular observations and broader cellular phenotypes.
- The importance of distinguishing an observed change from a proposed mechanism.

A recurring reasoning principle was that one result rarely establishes an entire mechanism.

For example, detecting two proteins in the same complex supports an association under the assay conditions, but does not by itself prove direct binding.

3. Experimental methods discussed

The following methods were covered conceptually, including what they are used for and how their results should be interpreted.

Protein interaction and detection methods

Co-immunoprecipitation (co-IP)

Used to test whether a protein is recovered in a complex with another protein under specified lysis and assay conditions. Discussion included the limitations of inferring direct binding from co-IP.

Pull-down assays

Used to examine interactions involving an immobilized bait and a potential binding partner. Interpretation depends on assay design and controls.

Western blotting

Used to detect specific proteins in a sample, with attention to loading, transfer, antibody specificity, and appropriate normalization.

Phospho-Western blotting

Used to examine phosphorylation of a target protein. A phospho-specific signal should be interpreted alongside total target protein where appropriate.

Cell-based and imaging methods

Immunofluorescence (IF)

Discussed as a way to examine the localization and distribution of proteins in cells, with attention to staining controls and image interpretation.

GFP-based approaches

Discussed in the context of visualizing or tracking a labelled protein or cellular process. A fluorescent signal must be interpreted in light of expression, localization, and construct-related limitations.

Flow cytometry

Discussed as a method for measuring properties of individual cells in a population.

Annexin V/PI staining

Discussed as a flow-cytometry approach for distinguishing cell populations with different membrane and cell-death-associated staining patterns. Interpretation depends on controls, gating, and biological context.

Separation and analytical methods

Chromatography

Discussed as a family of separation methods in which components distribute differently between stationary and mobile phases.

Protein solubility

Considered in relation to sample preparation, extraction, and the interpretation of recovered protein.

HPLC

Discussed as a high-performance liquid chromatography approach for separating and analysing mixture components.

Mass spectrometry

Discussed as an analytical method that measures mass-to-charge ratios and can support identification or characterization of molecules, depending on the workflow.

PCR and primer logic

PCR primer orientation

Examined using antiparallel DNA strands and the requirement that DNA polymerase extends from a primer's 3′ end in the 5′→3′ direction.

Forward and reverse primers

Discussed in relation to the reference-strand sequence, complementary binding, and inward-facing extension.

Amplicon boundaries

Explored through the insight that polymerase does not recognize the desired gene boundary as a stop signal. The primer positions establish the defined product boundaries through successive cycles of synthesis and template reuse.

Role of both primers

Considered in relation to efficient, exponential amplification of a specific bounded region.

This PCR topic was still being actively explored when this retrospective record was created. It should be treated as a learning area in progress rather than a mastered topic.

4. Experimental design and interpretation principles

A substantial part of the learning focused on reasoning across experiments rather than memorizing protocol names.

Controls

Topics included:

- Why a control is needed and what alternative explanation it addresses.
- Positive and negative controls.
- Input and loading controls in protein assays.
- The importance of appropriate controls for interaction assays and cell-based measurements.
- Why a control must match the question and the specific assay design.

Normalization

Discussion included:

- Why raw signal intensity may not be directly comparable between samples.
- The purpose of normalization.
- The importance of choosing a normalization strategy appropriate for the measurement.
- Why normalization does not automatically remove every source of bias or technical variation.

Validation and causal reasoning

Topics included:

- Rescue experiments and how they can strengthen a causal interpretation.
- Orthogonal validation: testing a conclusion using a different method or measurement principle.
- The distinction between correlation, association, and causation.
- The need to consider alternative explanations before accepting a mechanistic conclusion.
- The difference between evidence consistent with a hypothesis and evidence that uniquely supports it.

5. Reasoning skills practised

The learning sessions repeatedly returned to questions such as:

- What biological question is this experiment trying to answer?
- What does the method directly measure?
- What does the result allow us to conclude?
- What does it not establish?
- Which control would address a particular alternative explanation?
- Could a change in the measured signal reflect a change in total protein, sample loading, cell number, or another factor?
- What additional experiment would distinguish between competing explanations?
- Does the evidence support the authors' conclusion, or is the conclusion stronger than the data?

An important developing distinction was between knowing the name or definition of a technique and being able to decide when and why to use it.

6. Learning approach

The working approach has been retrieval-first and application-focused:

1. Attempt to explain or solve a question before receiving the full explanation.
2. Identify what is already understood and where the reasoning breaks down.
3. Clarify the relevant biological or technical principle.
4. Apply it to an experimental scenario.
5. Revisit related ideas when useful, without unnecessarily repeating entire explanations.
6. Build toward interpreting the logic of published experiments.

An analogy that helped frame this goal was language learning: recognizing vocabulary is not the same as being able to use it fluently.

Similarly, recognizing scientific terms is not the same as applying them to experimental decisions.

7. Current learning priorities

Based on the topics visible in the available history, useful continuing priorities include:

- Continue developing PCR understanding, especially primer orientation, amplicon boundaries, and the role of both primers.
- Strengthen the ability to select assays based on the biological question.
- Practise choosing controls and explaining exactly what each control rules out.
- Improve interpretation of results involving total protein, phosphorylated protein, protein interactions, and cell phenotypes.
- Practise identifying limitations and alternative explanations.
- Begin structured analyses of published papers, reconstructing the logic from research question to experimental design, results, interpretation, and limitations.
- Revisit earlier problems periodically to test retention and transfer to unfamiliar scenarios.

9. Overall reflection

The central development goal is to move from remembering scientific information toward using it to make and defend experimental decisions.

The repository will document that development honestly over time.

Guiding principle: Learn seriously. Reason carefully. Document honestly. Build the portfolio from the work itself.
