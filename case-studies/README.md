Experimental Reasoning Case Studies

This folder is dedicated to developing experimental reasoning through hypothetical biological research scenarios.

The purpose is to practise turning a biological question into an experimental strategy.

These exercises are conceptual unless explicitly based on a cited published study. Hypothetical experiments and results must never be presented as real laboratory work.

Structure of a case study

Each case study should include:

1. Research question

What biological problem are we trying to investigate?

2. Background

What is already known about the biological process?

3. Competing hypotheses

What different explanations could account for the observation?

4. Experimental strategy

Which experiments could distinguish between these explanations?

5. Method selection

Why is each method appropriate? What does it directly measure?

6. Controls

Which controls are needed, and what alternative explanations do they address?

7. Possible outcomes

What results might be obtained?

8. Interpretation

What would each possible result support or fail to establish?

9. Alternative explanations

Could another biological or technical factor explain the result?

10. Follow-up experiment

What additional experiment would strengthen the conclusion or distinguish competing mechanisms?

Possible case-study themes

- Investigating whether two proteins interact.
- Distinguishing protein association from direct binding.
- Examining whether a treatment changes EGFR phosphorylation.
- Investigating a possible relationship between vimentin and cell migration.
- Distinguishing increased cell death from reduced cell proliferation.
- Choosing between Western blotting, immunofluorescence, flow cytometry, and other methods based on the research question.

Main objective

The goal is not to select as many techniques as possible.

The goal is to select experiments that answer the research question, produce interpretable evidence, and address plausible alternative explanations.
